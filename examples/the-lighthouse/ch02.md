# 2. Low Water

By morning the light was gone, and so was most of Nell's certainty that she had seen it.

She went down to the harbour anyway. The tide was out, and the boats lay tilted on the mud like toys a child had grown tired of. Tom Rennick was mending a net on the slipway, his big hands moving slowly and patiently through the mesh.

"You're up early," he said, without looking up.

"I saw something last night. Out past the reef."

"Lots of things out past the reef." He tied off a knot. "Fog. Gulls. Ghosts, if you ask my mother."

"It was a light. A white one. It was moving."

Tom stopped working then. He looked at her for a long moment, and something passed across his face that she could not name, something that was perhaps fear or perhaps only tiredness, or perhaps a mixture of the two that she had no word for.

"Your father used to say that," he said quietly.

Nell felt the cold of the morning go right through her coat. Her father had been dead for eleven years. He had gone out in the lifeboat on the night of the October storm and had not come back, and in all that time nobody in Carrow had ever told her that he had seen anything at all.

"Say what?"

"That there was a light out there that wasn't a boat." Tom bent back to his net, but his hands had stopped being patient. "He wrote it down, I think. In the log. Whatever happened to the log, I couldn't tell you."

Nell could. It was in a tin box under her bed, where it had been since the funeral, and she had never once opened it.

She walked home very fast, and she did not count her steps.
