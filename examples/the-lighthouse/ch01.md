# 1. The Keeper's Daughter

The lighthouse at Carrow Point had not been lit for eleven years, but Nell Harlan climbed its stairs every evening anyway, counting the steps out loud the way her father had taught her.

There were one hundred and twelve of them. On the forty-first the iron rang a different note, hollow and slightly sad, and she always paused there to listen to it, as if the tower were trying to tell her something it had never quite managed to say.

At the top the great lens sat under its canvas hood like a sleeping animal. She did not lift the hood. She sat on the narrow gallery with her back against the cold glass and watched the sea go grey, then darker grey, then black.

The village below had mostly forgotten the lighthouse. The ferry used the new beacon on the breakwater, a small red light that blinked on a timer and needed nobody. Mrs. Abbot at the post office said it was progress. Nell said nothing, because she had learned that saying nothing was usually the thing that kept the peace in Carrow, and peace in Carrow was something that everyone very much wanted to keep.

That evening, though, something was different. Far out past the reef, where no boat had any business being at that hour, a light was moving. It was not red and it did not blink. It was white and steady, and it was coming slowly towards the shore.

Nell stood up very quickly and gripped the rail.

She knew every vessel that worked these waters, the trawlers and the crabbers and the summer yachts that came and went like swallows. This was none of them. It rode too low and too still, and it made no sound at all.

Below her, in the dark, the forty-first step rang once, by itself.
